Building a Cost-Per-Trip Model for Plastic Pallets

Heavydutyplasticpallets Editorial • October 2026

Unit price is the wrong metric for comparing pallets, yet it still dominates too many purchasing decisions. A heavy duty plastic pallet costs several times more than a stringer wood pallet at the point of sale, and on that basis wood wins every time. But pallets are not consumed in a single use—they make trips, often hundreds of them. Cost-per-trip analysis reframes the decision around the metric that actually governs your budget. This guide builds the model procurement teams use to justify plastic.

The Core Formula

Cost per trip is the total lifetime cost of a pallet divided by the number of trips it completes before retirement. Total lifetime cost includes purchase price plus the costs it drives over its life—freight, labor, product loss, disposal, and compliance. A wood pallet at a low purchase price but a short life and heavy hidden costs can easily cost more per trip than a plastic pallet priced several times higher. The analysis only works when you capture every cost category, not just the sticker.

Service Life Is the Biggest Lever

Trip count dominates the equation. Wood pallets in demanding closed-loop systems often survive only a handful of cycles before boards split and nails loosen; each repair or replacement resets the clock. Heavy duty plastic pallets routinely complete many times more trips without repair, which spreads their higher purchase price across a far larger denominator. When you evaluate the heavy duty plastic pallets, ask for the expected cycle count in an application like yours and use the conservative end of the range in your model.

Freight and Weight

In cold-chain and humid environments, wood absorbs water and gains weight, and that weight rides on every shipment as freight cost. Plastic pallets hold constant weight because they absorb no moisture. Over thousands of shipments, consistent lighter weight compounds into real freight savings that rarely appear on a pallet quote but belong squarely in the cost-per-trip model.

  • Purchase price spread across lifetime trips
  • Freight impact of moisture weight gain
  • Labor for repair, sorting, and disposal
  • Product loss from pallet failure in racks or transit
  • Compliance and audit costs for food and pharma

Labor, Loss, and Compliance

Wood pallets demand sorting, repair, and nail removal—labor that plastic largely eliminates. Pallet failure in a rack or in transit can destroy product worth far more than the pallet itself, so the reliability of a consistent molded pallet avoids loss events that swamp any unit-price difference. In regulated environments, pallets molded from FDA-compliant resin simplify audits and reduce contamination findings. Running the Numbers

Build a simple spreadsheet with two columns, wood and plastic. Enter purchase price, expected trips, per-trip freight delta from weight, annual repair and sorting labor, estimated loss events, and compliance costs. Divide total lifetime cost by trips for each. Most buyers find that plastic wins decisively in closed-loop, cold-chain, and automated applications, while wood may still suit one-way export where the pallet is not recovered. The model tells you which scenario you are in rather than leaving it to a gut call on sticker price.
