FDA-Compliant Plastic Pallets for Food Manufacturing: What Procurement Teams Need to Know

Heavydutyplasticpallets Editorial • September 2026

Why Compliance Matters in Pallet Selection

For food manufacturers, processors, distributors, and cold chain logistics operators, pallet selection is not simply a procurement decision — it carries regulatory implications. Pallets function as direct or indirect contact surfaces for food products across many operations, and regulatory bodies including the FDA and USDA have clear expectations for materials used in food processing and distribution environments. Getting this specification wrong creates audit exposure, potential recall liability, and operational disruption that far exceeds any unit cost savings from choosing non-compliant materials.

This guide covers the compliance requirements relevant to plastic pallet sourcing for food industry procurement teams.

FDA Compliance for Food-Contact Plastic Materials

The FDA regulates materials that come into direct or indirect contact with food under 21 CFR (Code of Federal Regulations). For plastic pallets used in food applications, the central requirement is that constituent materials must be composed of substances that are generally recognized as safe or approved under FDA food contact notifications.

For polypropylene pallets specifically, key compliance requirements include:

  • Virgin PP resin must be used — recycled blends may contain contaminants from prior use and are not acceptable for food contact applications
  • Resin must comply with FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 covering olefin polymers for food contact applications
  • Colorants and additives must also be FDA-approved for food contact — not all pigments or UV stabilizers meet this standard
  • Suppliers should provide a food contact compliance letter or certificate of conformance referencing the specific resin grade used

An important clarification: the pallet itself is not independently FDA approved as a finished product. Rather, the materials that compose it must comply with FDA food contact regulations. When a supplier claims FDA-approved pallets, they should mean that the resin, colorants, and additives are compliant with FDA food contact requirements — and that claim should be backed by documentation.

USDA Requirements in Federally Inspected Facilities

Meat and poultry processing facilities operating under USDA Federal Inspection are subject to additional requirements. USDA inspectors assess facility equipment and materials for cleanability and food safety compatibility. For pallets used in USDA-inspected facilities:

  • Plastic pallets are strongly preferred over wood, which cannot be reliably sanitized and is known to harbor pathogens in crevices, nail holes, and grain irregularities
  • Closed-deck plastic pallets are easier to sanitize than open-deck designs and are preferred for direct meat-proximity or contact applications
  • Pallets must withstand repeated wash-down cycles, including hot water and chemical sanitizer exposure, without degradation
  • Materials must not impart odors, flavors, or contaminants to food products at any point in their operational life

While USDA does not publish a formal approved pallet list, the agency's food safety requirements effectively mandate plastic over wood in fresh meat, poultry, and ready-to-eat food processing environments. Many plant auditors treat the presence of wood pallets in direct food-contact zones as an automatic corrective action finding.

Cleaning and Sanitization Protocol Compatibility

Compliance with FDA and USDA requirements is an ongoing operational requirement, not just a one-time purchase verification. Plastic pallets must be compatible with the facility's sanitization protocols throughout their working life:

  • Hot water and steam cleaning: PP pallets are generally rated to withstand wash temperatures up to 150–160°F without warping or dimensional distortion
  • Chemical sanitizers: PP is compatible with quaternary ammonium compounds, chlorine-based sanitizers, and peroxyacetic acid solutions at standard food-industry use concentrations
  • Pressure washing: Closed-deck PP pallets withstand pressure washing without structural damage; open-deck designs require attention to the understructure to ensure complete residue removal
  • Rapid reuse readiness: PP does not absorb moisture, so pallets air-dry quickly after wash-down — an operational advantage in high-throughput food facilities where pallet turnaround time affects productivity

Cold Chain Performance Considerations

For refrigerated and frozen food applications, pallet performance at low temperatures adds another specification layer. Standard PP performs adequately in refrigerated environments, but procurement teams sourcing for freezer applications should verify:

  • Minimum service temperature ratings — most heavy duty PP pallets are rated to 0°F or below for frozen applications
  • Impact resistance at cold temperatures — PP can become brittle below freezing, so quality cold chain pallets use impact-modified PP formulations
  • Dimensional stability across temperature cycles — pallets must maintain consistent dimensions moving between freezer, dock, and ambient environments without warping that would cause racking fit issues

Supplier Qualification Checklist for Food Industry Buyers

When qualifying plastic pallet suppliers for food industry use, request and verify the following documentation before placing orders:

  • FDA food contact compliance documentation citing 21 CFR 177.1520 for the specific resin grade used
  • Material Safety Data Sheet for the resin and any colorants or additives
  • Quality management system certification — ISO 9001 or equivalent
  • Lot traceability capability for resin materials used in production
  • References from current food industry customers using the same product configuration

Sourcing heavy duty plastic pallets from a direct manufacturer who maintains this documentation is significantly preferable to sourcing through distributors who may lack visibility into original resin sourcing or compliance certification status.

The Cost of Non-Compliance

Using non-compliant materials in a food production or distribution environment creates quantifiable financial risk: FDA Form 483 observations during inspections, USDA corrective action findings, product recalls, and customer supply chain audit failures. For procurement teams in regulated food environments, investing in properly documented, FDA-compliant plastic pallets is not an optional quality upgrade — it is a baseline requirement that protects the entire operation from regulatory and commercial exposure.
